The first chart makes it look as if favoritism grew a lot. Most of that comes from changes to the points system.

The second chart uses a measure the points system cannot touch. Bloc voting did grow: it rose once viewers voted, peaked in 2008 and fell when juries came back.

The third chart splits the years since 2016. Most of the bias that is left comes from the viewers.

Absolute favoritism is how far each vote is from what the receiving country got on average that year, whether above or below. The line is the average over all votes in a year. This is the measure from our Tableau work.

The strip underneath shows how many points each country handed out. Twice the points gives twice the deviation, even when nobody votes more for their friends. Hover a period in the strip to read its rules.

Same scale every year divides each year by the standard deviation of that year's points. After that the periods are almost level.

For every pair of countries, this year's favoritism is compared with the pair's average over the previous five contests (pairs that met at least twice in them). The line is the correlation: at 0 past votes say nothing about this year's, at 1 everyone votes exactly as before. A correlation does not care how big the points are.

Dealing out every country's points at random gives 0.00. The dashed line keeps only the countries that were in finals before 2004, in 2004–2015 and after 2016, so new countries joining cannot explain the rise.

The vertical lines mark rule changes: televoting in most countries from 1998, semi-finals in 2004, juries back alongside viewers in 2009, separate jury and televote points in 2016.

Since 2016 jury and televote points are published separately. Each line shows how well that year's jury (or televote) favoritism matches every pair's average from 2004–2015 (pairs that met at least three times).

On average the televote matches at 0.54 and the juries at 0.22. Viewers follow the old friendships about 2.5 times as closely, in every single year.
