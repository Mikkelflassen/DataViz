      Favoritism is the points one country gives another, minus the average the recipient received from all voters that year, averaged over their shared finals.

      With no country selected, the map shows how many points each country received per final: per voting country, compared with the average entry in the same final, and averaged over the finals it was in (with the same minimum number of finals as for pairs). Select a country in the list or on the map to see its favoritism; click it again to go back.

      Finals only, 1957–2023; no contest in 2020. Minimum shared finals per pair: 5 for ranges of 20+ years, 3 for 8–19 years, 1 otherwise.

      Symmetric log scale: linear near zero and logarithmic beyond ±1, so both small and large values remain distinguishable. Red and blue are symmetric.

      The same scale is used with and without a selected country. Without one, red means more points per final than the average entry, blue fewer.

      Micro-states are shown as dots; Australia and former states in the inset boxes. Grey countries were in no final in the selected years.

      Pairs ranked by mutual favoritism (mean of both directions). Each bar shows the points given by the country on that side, on a shared scale from zero.

      Hover a row to locate the pair on the map.
