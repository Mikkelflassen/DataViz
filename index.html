      Strongest friendships 1957–2023

      Pick a country in the list or click it on the map. Every other country is coloured by its favoritism: how many more or fewer points it gives than expected.

      Expected = the average points the receiving country got from all voters that year. So +10 means about ten extra points every final the two met in, and a song everyone liked doesn't count as favoritism.

      Who favours it colours the countries that give points to the selected country. Who it favours colours the countries it gives points to.

      The page opens on the country you are visiting from, using your browser's time zone and language. Add #dk, #se etc. to the address to open on another country.

      Only finals are counted, 1957–2023 (no contest in 2020). The "rest of world" televote is left out.

      A pair needs enough shared finals to be reliable: at least 5 for ranges of 20 years or more, 3 for 8–19 years and 1 for shorter ranges. Pairs below that are hatched.

      The presets are the eras of the voting system: 12-point system from 1975, semi-finals from 2004, separate jury and televote from 2016.

      The colours use a symmetric log scale (symlog). Half of all country pairs are within ±1 point, while a few reach +13. On a straight scale the small ones all look grey and the big ones get cut off.

      Symlog behaves like a normal scale close to 0 and like a log scale further out, so both 0.5 vs 2 and 5 vs 13 stay visible. A plain log scale can't be used because the values can be 0 or negative.

      The scale is the same on both sides: equally strong red and blue mean the same number of points.

      Micro-states (Andorra, Monaco, San Marino, Malta, Luxembourg) are drawn as dots.

      Australia, Yugoslavia and Serbia & Montenegro sit in the boxes on the left. Grey countries never took part. Hover any country for its exact value.

      Each row is a pair of countries, ranked by how much they favour each other on average.

      Each bar is what that side's country gives the other. Equal bars mean a mutual friendship; one long and one short bar mean it is one-sided.

      Hover a row to find the two countries on the map, click it to select one. With … shows only pairs that include the selected country.
